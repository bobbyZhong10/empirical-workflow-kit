# Outlet Positioning Reference

Use this reference in Stage 2 to make outlet choice falsifiable rather than
aspirational. Each candidate row must cite verified sources in the literature
map; do not infer a journal's standards from a single paper. Update the row
when the contribution, theory, or design changes.

| Target outlet | Framing anchor | Theory source | Empirical analogue | Method authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Management Science | State the management or market-design conversation and the decision consequence. | Verified theory paper(s) that define the mechanism and boundary condition. | Closest verified setting, unit, outcome, and contribution. | Verified design or econometric source governing the identifying claim. |
| Information Systems Research | State the information-systems phenomenon and organizational or societal consequence. | Verified IS or adjacent theory source with a stated causal mechanism. | Closest verified IS study, including what differs in setting or measure. | Verified method authority plus outlet-relevant identification precedent. |
| MIS Quarterly | State the IS-theory contribution, construct novelty, and why the setting tests it. | Verified theory source and construct definition. | Closest verified empirical study and the precise gap. | Verified causal-design authority, diagnostic precedent, and reporting standard. |

## Completion rules

- Replace every placeholder with a citation key and stable verified source
  location before using the row in a manuscript or outlet-positioning memo.
- The framing anchor names a research conversation, not a broad topic or a
  journal reputation.
- Theory sources explain the proposed mechanism; empirical analogues establish
  the closest observed comparison; method authorities justify design and
  diagnostics. Do not use one citation as a substitute for all three roles.
- Record the selected target and the reasons alternatives were rejected in the
  literature map and decision log. A change in target that changes the
  contribution framing requires the protocol's recorded decision.
