# Research Writing

Apply these rules to papers, research memos, reviews, response letters, slide
content, code comments, and other durable research artifacts. Conversation
formatting belongs to the active runtime and is not governed here.

## Organize around the question and the knowledge gained

This contract applies to every evidence strategy and to methods paragraphs,
results, abstracts, discussions, memos, and research talks. Method-specific
instructions supply technical details, not an alternative paper outline.

Begin by making the setting intelligible: who acts or is affected, what choices,
information and constraints matter, and which economic uncertainty, tradeoff,
or conflict motivates the question. Do not invent strategic actors or a conflict
for a measurement or methodological paper. Explain the unknown and its consequence.
Then state what evidence can answer it and what was learned. Build the manuscript
around a small sequence of questions the reader must resolve to understand that
answer. Each section and exhibit has a job in that argument; estimator order,
hypothesis numbering, diagnostic inventories, and the chronology of discovery do
not dictate the structure. A separate theory or mechanism section is warranted
only when it advances the argument. Drafting order is not reader-facing order.

Separate three steps without requiring three repetitive labels in prose:
the finding (what the evidence establishes), its economic meaning (what changes
for whom and at what relevant scale), and its contribution (which prior belief,
explanation, measurement, or decision this changes). State what the closest
research already establishes and the precise increment here. A new dataset,
setting, estimator, significant coefficient, or method combination is not by
itself that increment. Credible measurement, description, institutional
accounting, a well-supported null, a boundary condition, and conditional model
analysis can supply it. Do not force each into theoretical novelty or causal
identification. A main contribution can have complementary findings; neither a
quota nor a repeated contribution sentence establishes coherence.

When evidence is stable, explain the contribution it actually supports. Earlier
plans constrain honest provenance, not the answer the paper must reach. If the
increment is only useful background, demote it; if no important uncertainty is
resolved, reconsider the question or stop. External readers assess interest.

## Economic meaning and implications

Interpret important results in the units, population, comparison, and horizon of
the estimand. Choose a benchmark that answers the economic question, explain its
relevance, and retain uncertainty in the conversion. Distinguish absolute from
relative changes, percentages from percentage points, levels from elasticities,
individual averages from totals, and short-run from long-run outcomes. A scale
conversion cannot expand a local population, observed sample, or supported range.
Do not convert an association into an intervention benefit by multiplying it by
a population or price. Use only the magnitude and precision the reader needs.

Explain whose behavior, resources, outcomes, or opportunities change. Gross
revenue, accounting payments, profits, surplus, and welfare are different
objects; costs, transfers, distribution, outside options, and equilibrium
responses may matter. Managerial or policy recommendations need an implementable
choice, relevant objective and costs, and an evidence-supported comparison. If
these are absent, state a bounded implication or conditional scenario. Not every
paper needs a welfare calculation, prescription, or managerial paragraph.

## Behavioral and mechanism explanations

Distinguish observed actions from inferred motives, beliefs, preferences,
attention, learning, constraints, and strategic intent. Explain which actors
could respond, what changes for them, the predicted observable pattern, and the
competing explanations. A mechanism-consistent sign, subgroup interaction, text
label, self-report, or fitted parameter does not on its own uniquely identify a
behavioral process. Do not tell an optimizing, psychological, or strategic story
merely because it can rationalize the result. Keep such a story as a plausible
interpretation when alternatives remain indistinguishable.

Treatment assignment, measurement, and behavioral mechanisms answer different
questions. Randomizing treatment does not automatically identify a mediator or
motive. Adding a post-treatment variable, finding coefficient attenuation, or
splitting by a post-treatment outcome does not establish mediation. A formal
mediation claim needs its own identification argument and appropriate method;
if the current method pack does not support it, record the missing analysis and
route through method governance rather than inventing an implementation.

For a behavioral model, distinguish maintained assumptions from implications
that the data test. Fit to targeted moments cannot independently confirm the
behavioral assumptions that produced them. Untargeted evidence can discriminate
only among the alternatives it actually challenges. An estimated parameter has
its behavioral meaning conditional on the model and identification argument;
calibration, simulation, or predictive success cannot silently strengthen that
interpretation. Preserve useful conditional explanations without calling them
observed motives.

## Apply the boundary to the selected evidence strategy

Use the relevant row as an interpretation prompt, not a new mandatory battery.
Technical diagnostics remain in the selected pack; these examples do not extend
its supported methods or replace a current method authority.

| Evidence strategy | Interpretation and contribution question |
|---|---|
| Measurement, description, institutional accounting | What population and construct are captured? Does the new fact change knowledge beyond the measurement rule? Records, proxies, and identities do not establish individual behavior. |
| Fixed effects and selection on observables | What variation/population survives adjustment or overlap restriction? Conditional association and causal interpretation require different arguments; balance and predictive fit cannot establish absence of unmeasured confounding. |
| DiD, event study, DDD | Which contrast, weights, window and counterfactual support the result? Dynamic patterns and shared comparisons do not uniquely identify anticipation, adaptation, or a mechanism. |
| IV and fuzzy RD | Which affected population and treatment margin does the instrument identify under its assumptions? Assignment mechanism, treatment mechanism and population-wide policy effect are distinct. |
| Sharp RD and local randomization | What can be learned near the cutoff or within the justified window? A local effect does not automatically describe other thresholds or an overall policy change. |
| Synthetic control and related panel counterfactuals | What treated case and time path are compared? Fit and placebo diagnostics do not independently resolve common shocks, mechanism, or transport to other cases. |
| Field experiments | What assignment effect is identified for the experimental sample? Receipt, mediation, attrition, spillovers, scale and longer-run outcomes need their own evidence or assumptions. |
| Conjoint and stated-preference measurement | What task, attributes, profile distribution and choice object are studied? Task effects, modeled preferences, realized uptake and market welfare are distinct; use the pack's existing claims firewall. |
| Prediction, text/ML measurement, heterogeneity and policy learning within a supported pack | What construct, held-out target, loss or policy value is validated? Accuracy is not causal identification; feature importance is not mechanism, and subgroup prediction is not a proven motive or deployable policy gain. |
| Structural estimation and conditional models | What comes from data, identification, calibration, or maintained behavior? Parameter meaning, counterfactual responses and welfare remain conditional on their respective assumptions and support. |

## Claims and effects

- Every substantive claim needs a citation, a registered result, an observed
  fact, or an argument stated in the artifact. Delete an unsupported assertion
  or label it as the author's judgment with its rationale.
- An effect statement gives its direction, magnitude, and a benchmark in units
  the audience understands: currency, a baseline share, a standard deviation,
  or another justified scale. Statistical significance does not describe
  practical importance.
- Keep uncertainty that changes interpretation. Use direct statements for
  settled points and calibrated language for imprecise or conditional results.
- Follow an abstract method statement with a concrete instance. A claim of
  breadth names the covered domains and an example from each.

## Limitations and deviations

- Put a limitation where the relevant design, measurement, model, or result is
  introduced. A later limitations section may consolidate those constraints
  but may not be their first appearance.
- Use first person for an authorial choice. Name its price in statistical
  power, identification, generalizability, construct validity, or model
  dependence.
- Separate data limitations from method limitations. Record the affected claim
  and propagate the restriction to the title, abstract, introduction,
  discussion, and conclusion.
- Disclose material deviations affecting interpretation, including reason and
  timing relative to outcome inspection. Keep identifiers and the full decision
  history in internal records; do not narrate routine revisions in the paper.

## Sources, summaries, and quotations

- Report a source in indirect speech and in original wording. Text retained
  verbatim is quoted and carries a page, section, table, figure, theorem, or
  other stable locator.
- State whether the version read was the version of record, a preprint, a
  working paper, or an abstract. Do not describe an abstract as if full text
  was read.
- Preserve source punctuation inside a quotation. Never invent a DOI, page,
  quotation, bibliographic field, or citation key.
- Write the final deliverable once after its evidence and structure are
  settled. Scratch reasoning is not copied into the artifact as filler.

## Direct prose

- Prefer prose for connected arguments and lists for genuinely enumerable
  items. Avoid bold labels scattered through paragraphs, title-case headings,
  decorative emphasis, and emoji in research artifacts.
- Avoid negative parallelism such as "not just X, but Y". State the positive
  proposition directly.
- Do not manufacture a three-part slogan. Preserve a genuine enumeration that
  happens to contain three items.
- Remove trailing editorial clauses that announce what the sentence is doing.
  Give a separate sentence to a real claim.
- Prefer full stops to semicolons. Avoid em dashes in authored prose; preserve
  them in quotations and source data.
- Start a paragraph for a new idea. Use one claim per sentence unless one long
  sentence is carrying one continuous argument.
- Prefer plain verbs and stable terminology. Repeat the technical noun instead
  of cycling through near-synonyms. Unpack noun stacks with verbs and
  prepositions.
- Remove conversational scaffolding and assurance language from durable work.
  Begin with the finding, decision, problem, or requested content.
- Avoid mannered metaphors when a literal description is available.

## Editing restraint

Leave sound prose alone. Do not flag a formal term, a long sentence carrying
one argument, a genuine three-item enumeration, source punctuation, or a
technical expression merely because it is stylistically marked. When editing
an author's prose, report what changed and why and provide a diff when the
runtime supports it.

Common model-generated editorial words to remove unless they carry a precise
technical meaning include: delve, tapestry, testament, underscore, showcase,
pivotal, crucial, seamless, meticulous, boasts, notably, importantly,
effectively, carefully, particularly, and crucially. "Significantly" is
reserved for statistical significance.

## The paper is an argument, not an audit record

Keep prediction counts and IDs, item-by-item pass/fail summaries, stages, gates,
registry and ledger vocabulary, defenses of unused methods, and immaterial
exploration or repair history out of the manuscript by default. A reader-facing
methods explanation or material preregistration deviation is warranted when it
changes interpretation, not because it exists in an internal record. Appendices
also need a reader purpose; they are not a dumping ground for audit history.

Select numbers for the argument, magnitude, and uncertainty. Keep exact values,
CSV rows, and provenance internally; round consistently for readers. Traceability
is a backend requirement, not an obligation to display more numbers. State a
condition where it governs the result and keep standalone summaries accurate;
do not repeat the same defensive clause at every mention.

## Check propositions against evidence

For each important claim, identify its basis and the inferential bridge in the
internal claim-to-evidence audit: observation unit, construct, estimand, shared
inputs, bridge assumptions, and the strongest warranted sentence. Link existing
specifications rather than creating another ledger. Inspect the evidence and
assumptions, not just its label. A registry status of `supported` does not turn
exploration into confirmation or supply identification. Correct understatement
as well as overstatement; prefer the accurate affirmative finding. Apply these boundaries to the exact proposition, including paraphrases:

| Evidence in hand | Unsupported leap | Defensible statement or evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Aggregate net changes | Individuals migrated between platforms | Describe net changes; individual migration needs linked transitions and coverage assumptions. |
| A variable inverted from the outcome or an identity, such as login hours reconstructed from trips and utilization | Independent mechanism confirmation or direct login records | State the reconstruction and shared inputs; mechanism evidence needs independent variation or measurement. |
| Waiting time among completed trips | Welfare of all potential riders | Describe the observed trips; requests, cancellations, abandonment, selection, and valuation require additional evidence or explicit assumptions. |
| Changes in administrative records | Changes in real available supply | Describe recorded units; establish duplication, coverage, and availability before claiming supply. |
| Formula payment under a counterfactual input | Identified income or welfare losses | State a conditional payment calculation and fixed inputs; earnings and welfare need behavioral, hours, cost, and equilibrium assumptions as applicable. |
| A contemporaneous policy bundle | Effect of a single component or information channel | Interpret the bundle unless variation separates its components. |

Use accurate verbs and necessary conditions in the paper, not repeated
'observational', 'descriptive', or 'model-based' badges. A mechanically matched
anchor or a passed lexical screen does not validate these bridges.
